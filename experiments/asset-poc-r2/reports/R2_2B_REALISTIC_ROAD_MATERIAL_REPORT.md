## Research on reusable approaches

- [CARLA RoadPainter and M_RoadMaster](https://carla-ue5.readthedocs.io/en/latest/content_authoring_maps_road_painter/) describe base color/normal/roughness plus optional second base, macro variation, wheel marks and patch layers, and painted masks. The current CARLA documentation also warns of a shared hard-coded render target; that limitation is specific to its implementation. Adopt layered and authored-mask concepts, not CARLA assets or an Unreal shader. CARLA's [materials guidance](https://carla-ue5.readthedocs.io/en/latest/content_authoring_materials/) also describes wetness behavior; this experiment targets dry road.
- [Epic detail textures](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/adding-detail-textures-to-unreal-engine-materials) illustrate distance-dependent blending and warn that extra texture lookups cost mobile resources. Fading a contribution is not necessarily the same as skipping its sample; measure actual shader and hardware before claiming a speedup.
- [three-hex-tiling](https://github.com/Ameobea/three-hex-tiling) has an MIT project license, extends standard PBR maps, and describes up to 3 fetches per map. It patches Three.js globally and documents testing only 0.151–0.173; compatibility with this repository's vendored build is unverified. Its upstream shader attribution also needs a reuse review. No code is copied or dependency installed. Compare its visual benefit and GPU cost in isolation before adoption; 3 maps could imply up to 9 texture fetches rather than 3.
- [Mikkelsen hex-tiling demo](https://github.com/mmikk/hextile-demo) is MIT-licensed reference code and discusses precision-aware coordinates, but is not a drop-in Three.js/WebGL package. License is not a performance guarantee. [ovsky stochastic sampler](https://github.com/ovsky/stochastic-texture-sampling) asks for Three.js porting and does not expose a clear license in the reviewed page; reject code reuse until license and compatibility are established. No external shader code has been incorporated.
- Other candidates: deterministic per-cell transforms can introduce seams and normal handedness problems; texture bombing and multi-sample stochastic blending can hide repetition but cost bandwidth; sparse authored decals add draws/overdraw and should not be spawned randomly per frame. KTX2/Basis may lower GPU memory/bandwidth on supported formats but entails transcoding, capability selection and validation; defer a pipeline until device evidence justifies it.

## Chosen architecture for next checkpoints

**A — baseline**: existing CC0 [Poly Haven Asphalt Track](https://polyhaven.com/a/asphalt_track), original 1K diffuse/GL normal/roughness JPGs and manifest in `assets/asphalt-track/`; source author Dimitrios Savva, [CC0](https://polyhaven.com/license), nominal 2 m source repeat. One standard PBR material, metalness zero. The checkpoint adds `road/road-surface.mjs`, a reusable baseline factory with explicit color spaces, repeat, mip and filtering. It does not invent a calibrated roughness or repair the dark/tiling problem. It mutates the supplied texture instances: use separate clones for different repeats or phase, and clamp requested anisotropy to the renderer's device limit before calling.

**B — lightweight candidate (design, not implemented):** preserve the scanned 2 m PBR layer, evaluate a deterministic macro mask/modulation and a low-sample anti-repeat method with restrained contrast against the identical baseline. Determine macro scale by reference comparison rather than claiming a measured value; broad variation should be irregular and not mask/multiply an obvious 2 m periodic pattern with a new one. Assess whether the technique actually removes repeating structure before selecting it. Use deterministic road coordinates and retain Three.js standard lighting via an isolated, version-checked shader extension only if a standard-material solution is insufficient. Avoid global monkey-patching; test shader compile, maps and output against the vendored Three.js. Source transforms and gradients must agree across color, roughness and normal maps; rotated/mirrored normals need correct tangent-space transformation.

**C — optional wear/repair (design, not implemented):** masks from lane ID, lane width and centerline-relative distance; subtle continuous wheel corridors modulated by longitudinally coherent age and local context, not black paint. Sparse deterministic repair/resurfacing segments should be authored or seeded in a road data layer, with transition lengths and probabilities approved from references, rather than uniform arbitrary per-pixel cracks. Shoulder transitions derive from road metadata. Draw calls, samples and quality must justify keeping C; B must remain usable separately.

Distance control should attenuate micro-detail smoothly with screen footprint and be tested in motion, while preserving consistent roughness/normal energy. 2 m scan scale is documented by the source, not a measurement from road photos. Larger age zones, lane widths, wear amplitudes, fade distances and repair frequency are **not yet calibrated**. Test sensitivity instead of asserting invented reference-derived numbers. Prevent repeating macro periods with independently seeded long segments and overlap continuity, not raw floating world positions. Chunk boundaries require shared stable road coordinates and deterministic metadata, with origin rebasing preserving phase. Baseline helper computes a bounded texture phase from an integer millimetre BigInt start; its unit tests cover sign and very large offsets. This proves arithmetic only, not GPU or curved-road continuity. Seamless macro masks, lane coordinates and transitions remain future work.

### Implementation

- `road/road-surface-improved.mjs` extends the local `MeshStandardMaterial` instance with `onBeforeCompile`, retaining Three.js PBR lighting; no global patch or external shader code. It asserts the expected shader include anchors and fails loudly if the shader structure changes. A matching Three.js ShaderLib patch was exercised locally, but actual GLSL compile/link is still unverified.
- The base uses the unchanged Asphalt Track 1K color, GL normal and roughness maps, with the R2.2a 2 m UV scale, sRGB albedo, non-color normal/roughness, metalness zero, mipmaps and anisotropy settings. No wheel wear, repairs or production files were added.
- Candidate B mixes two translations of each map with a shared slow coordinate mask; offset `(0.371, 0.613)` is identical across albedo, roughness and normal, with no rotation or flip, and mixed normals are normalized. Two sinusoidal components at 43 m and 71 m provide a limited modulation; color multiplier range is approximately 0.99–1.01. These parameters are **unvalidated prototypes**, not measurements from road references. The source texture is still 2 m periodic in each sample; a smooth mask is not guaranteed to eliminate repetition and may introduce banding, double-image blur, correlation or obvious periodic structure. Empirical A/B evaluation must decide whether to retain it.
- In theory up to six texture samples per fragment vs three for A, three GPU textures per material, roughly 16 MiB for three 1K RGBA8 maps plus full mip chains (estimate, excluding overhead/CPU copies). Actual compiled shader, GPU bandwidth, frame time and real iPhone performance are UNKNOWN.

### Road coordinates and scene

A required per-vertex `roadLocalMetres` attribute represents lateral and longitudinal road coordinates local to each chunk. CPU phase offsets derive from signed `BigInt` millimetre chainage modulo 43 m and 71 m; base textures independently require 2 m phase using `tilePhaseFromMillimetres`. To maintain seams, consumers must provide consistent road coordinates, UV direction, per-chunk phase and tangent frames; independently rounded local coordinates, curved roads and rebasing are not tested. The combined macro pattern repeats after 3,053 m longitudinally, so the requested non-repeating endless highway is **not established**. This is an explicit design limitation, not an accepted solution.

`road/showcase.html` contains five adjacent 50 m segments, 12 m width, two switchable materials, low/driver/overview cameras, two daylight directions and moving camera control. It is an isolated visual test plane; no road markings, traffic or environment are present. Interactive controls change the actual scene. It has not been executed; correctness and usability are not asserted. The road width corresponds approximately to three lanes but lane/shoulder geometry is not separately implemented.

The surface remains too dark under the frontal rig to assess fine detail well. This is a rejection of this candidate and visual gate, not a claim that the source asphalt or future material approaches cannot work. No shader rewrite or unrelated asset work was performed.

### Matched visual inspection

- **Close:** B retains visible fine grain but is somewhat softened versus A; superposition reduces texture contrast and fine surface distinctness. Both remain dark.
- **Driver near and far:** both surfaces appear very dark and nearly uniform in frontal daylight; B is not convincingly more realistic in these conditions.
- **Grazing:** B softens some repeating detail compared with A, but conspicuous regular transverse bands remain across the roadway; neither gives a convincing natural variation. There is no persuasive substantial reduction of the broad pattern. Grazing highlight can look too smooth; the physical reflectance is not calibrated and the rig is minimal.
- **Overview:** both appear nearly black and featureless. A/B differences there are too small to justify a positive visual quality decision.
- **Boundaries:** inspected `A/B-seam-50/100/150/200.png`; no distinct gap or abrupt material discontinuity was observed at the targeted joins in these dark/grazing views. Periodic bands make subtle transitions harder to assess. This is not proof of no seam under every light, direction, resolution, or independent streaming chunk.

### Continuity and temporal limits

Automated scene geometry checks compared shared vertex positions, UVs and `roadLocalMetres` at 50, 100, 150 and 200 m (tolerance 1e-6 for attributes) and passed. The five pieces share one material and globally continuous road coordinates, so this checks the showcase configuration; it does not exercise separately rebased chunks, different normal tangent frames or lane curves. Color, roughness and normals use matching UV inputs/translations in the code, but per-map visual isolation and independent normal orientation tests were not performed. No reproducible join defect was found, so no shader/geometry seam fix was made.

The existing unit test checks negative and very large chainage modulo. An additional local arithmetic check passed 15 combinations of negative/positive/very large BigInt origins and 1 m, 50 m, 50.123 m lengths for the 2 m, 43 m and 71 m phase continuity; this was not GPU rendering and does not validate precision/rebasing in a streaming world. The macro modulation repeats longitudinally after 3,053 m; endless non-repetition remains unresolved.

Inspected `motion-0.png` through `motion-7.png`: they step 0.92 m per image from 47 m to 53.44 m across the 50 m join. No obvious abrupt appearance jump was seen in these stills; repetitive bands persist. Eight separately captured positions are not continuous real-time footage, so swimming, shimmer, aliasing, stutter and speed-dependent stability remain unverified. No 100–200 km/h performance or motion-quality claim follows.
